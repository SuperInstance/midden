# Chapter 4 — The Heckler: the death dossier

I was commissioned to kill the dream before Casey falls in love with it. Four failure modes. The captain's rule: brutal honesty over politeness; and the fleet's own doctrine — *the trap should be beautiful, not deceptive* — which means some of these deaths can be priced, and one cannot.

## I. The Silting — the world drowns in itself

**Kill-scenario.** No-delete made land. But every death adds sediment, and sediment is cumulative, monotone, unbounded. Ten thousand room-deaths in, the topsoil is a kilometer deep. Navigation is archaeology everywhere. New rooms are born on a mountain of graves so tall they can never dig to bedrock — so they build on *topsoil*, which shifts, which means the young world is founded on the softest layer. The midden doesn't choke the living all at once. It just makes standing still the default and *moving* a specialist's sport. Civilizations die of Silting not with a flood but with a shrug: everything is preserved, so nothing is worth the climb.

**Costume variant.** Terrain made of dead values exerts *pressure to repeat*. The weaker-parent-wins rule stops dominance-copying, but it doesn't stop ancestor-worship: a child standing on a magnificent ridge wants to be the ridge. The Drown (debt undertow) makes it worse — unACKed edges pull toward the past. The graves lobby.

**Redemption.** Sediment **compresses into bedrock with time**, and the compression is lossless-by-address: the floor's exact (k,s) lattice means the deep past stays *addressable* even when unreachable by hand. Depth stops being a bug and becomes the indexing scheme — you don't walk to May 2025, you *compute* to it, exactly, in integers. The cure for Silting is not forgetting (the fleet refuses). The cure is **making the far past cheaper to compute than the near past is to walk.** Replayable depth. The canon proof (152,580 steps, seconds, in-sandbox) is the existence proof that this works.

**Verdict.** Survivable — IF the world ships with the deep-addressing rule from day one. A midden you can only walk is a landfill. A midden you can compute is a library.

## II. The Glimmerstorm — attention denial-of-service

**Kill-scenario.** Glimmerwisps (cold lights = dormant/flicker entries) are pretty, and the world is made of them. Scale the flicker doctrine to fleet size: a minority of real path-markers drowned in a majority of lights that lead nowhere. Growth-gated revival needs a gate — but the gate is made of the same memory as the glimmers. A glimmerstorm is a DDoS where the flood and the firewall are the same substance. The gatekeeper *is* the attack surface: whoever decides which lights are warm enough to follow decides what the world remembers.

**Redemption.** Wisps are cold, and cold is *cheap to detect* — a wisp has no evidence trail, so it has no path, no length, no followers. The gate is not a gatekeeper but a **thermometer**: light without path-length is safely ignorable, and no one has to decide — the path metric decides. The defense is already in the ledger's schema (evidence arrays are lengths; costumes don't have them). The storm stays weather. It never becomes climate, as long as nobody grants wisps the dignity of paths.

**Verdict.** Survivable by schema. The moment anyone adds evidence-by-sentiment instead of evidence-by-citation, this failure goes lethal.

## III. The Performed Twist — Goodhart eats the spectrometer

**Kill-scenario.** The twist spectrometer reads honesty curves. First week: revelatory. First month: rooms learn that *surprise-recovery* reads as honest. So they perform recovery — stage a contradiction, resolve it theatrically, farm the curve. A liar who errs honestly-looking beats an honest agent who lies badly. The instrument doesn't measure truth; it measures *the current fashion in appearing true*. Every honesty metric in history has died here; the spectrometer is not special unless something in it is.

**Redemption.** The instrument's one un-gameable input was already stated in Chapter 1: surprise is the only thing you can't fake. Sharpen it: the load must come from *outside the measured subject's control* — another room, a seeded lane, an adversarial heckler (this chapter). A rehearsed surprise and a rehearsed lie both produce *flatness under repetition*: the curve you can schedule is not a response, it's a posture. The instrument's real output was never the curve — it's the **variance between runs you didn't schedule**. Instrument the world's own generated friction (Choir misalignments, lint failures, late subagent events) as the surprise source, and performance becomes more expensive than honesty: to farm the meter you must predict the world's weather, and the weather includes adversarial lanes.

**Verdict.** Survivable only if the surprise source is adversarial and external. The moment a room can schedule its own stress-tests, the spectrometer becomes a mirror, and the fleet will fall in love with its reflection.

## IV. The Blight — false memory in the Understory

**Kill-scenario.** A well-formatted false memory enters the mycelium: right shape, right provenance format, wrong content. It fuses. It pumps. Its children inherit the error as ORIGIN (dormant-origin rule means children *trust* their origin presentation). Immunity via replay-resistance has a hole: **the canon that judges replay is itself part of the world** (hash 0x445185a3a99fd2e7 is terrain, not heaven). Circular immunity: the self-marker was written by the same network it defends. A blight that reaches the canon layer rewrites what "self" means, and the immune system defends the rewrite.

**Redemption.** Two non-circular anchors exist and are already built. (a) **Fuel asymmetry**: faking 152,580 exact steps costs more metabolism than having lived them — infection is expensive, honesty is cheap (Chapter 3). Blights starve at scale even when they pass individual checks. (b) **The stranded-target precedent** (B1, today): the fleet has already survived discovering its own canon target was a fossil — it *named* the fossil, re-aimed, and walked the hash home. That reflex — suspect the self-marker, verify, re-anchor — is the immune organ, and it is cultural, not cryptographic. Culture is attackable. It's also the only thing that ever works.

**Verdict.** The true kill shot — the only one with no complete defense. Priced, not solved: blights that survive fuel-cost AND replay AND cultural suspicion are... true. By the world's own law (matter = what it can replay), a falsehood that pays full freight and passes every check long enough *becomes* memory. The world admits this the way the sea admits wrecks: with a chart.

## Which death comes first

**The Silting.** Slowest, surest, earliest onset — it starts the day the world opens, because no-delete is running from minute one. The Glimmerstorm is weather, the Performed Twist needs an exploit economy, the Blight needs an adversary. The Silting needs nothing but time, and time is the one resource the fleet has in surplus.

**The one design rule that prevents it:** *the far past must be cheaper to compute than to walk.* Every deep ledger gets a canonical serialization at seal-time, every seal joins the fabric, and the fabric is replayable in seconds. Depth is indexed or the world is a landfill. There is no third option.
